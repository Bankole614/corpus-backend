## 2. Concepts represented by the proposed additions

### Japanese / Irezumi
- **Card 25:** Dragon, waves, and flowing wind patterns.
- **Card 26:** Koi, maple leaves, and seasonal motifs.
- **Card 27:** Hannya mask framed by peonies.
- **Card 28:** Phoenix with traditional wind bars.
- Visual direction: strong outlines, intentional background flow, clear focal subject, and a composition designed to follow the body's shape.

### Neo-traditional
- **Card 29:** Ornate fox and peonies.
- **Card 30:** Medusa portrait and serpents.
- **Card 31:** Ganesha seated or framed with a lotus, with iconography researched for accuracy.
- **Card 32:** Sacred heart surrounded by roses.
- Visual direction: bold contours, decorative details, dimensional shading, and richer colors than a simple traditional flash design.

### Realism
- **Card 33:** Greco-Roman marble bust.
- **Card 34:** Realistic tiger portrait.
- **Card 35:** Black-and-grey portrait study.
- **Card 36:** Anatomical heart and vessels.
- Visual direction: convincing light, shadow, texture, anatomy, and tonal transitions. Distinguish realism from microrealism by avoiding an unnecessarily tiny or miniature presentation.

### Ornamental
- **Card 37:** Art Deco sunburst and moth.
- **Card 38:** Jain cosmological emblem.
- **Card 39:** Islamic geometric medallion.
- **Card 40:** Celtic interlace band.
- Visual direction: deliberate geometry, symmetry or radial balance, clean negative space, and crisp linework. Research the relevant symbol or cultural tradition before finalizing an image.

### Watercolor
- **Card 41:** Hummingbird and blossoms.
- **Card 42:** Koi with painterly color wash.
- **Card 43:** Dharmachakra and lotus.
- **Card 44:** Dove and olive branch.
- Visual direction: watercolor-like pigment washes, soft transitions, and selected splashes of color while keeping the main subject legible as a tattoo.

### Surrealism
- **Card 45:** Antique key with a floating landscape.
- **Card 46:** Melting hourglass and wildflowers.
- **Card 47:** Eye within a stone portal.
- **Card 48:** Classical bust with an unexpected blooming branch.
- Visual direction: dreamlike visual logic, meaningful juxtaposition, and clear focal hierarchy. Avoid turning every concept into generic fantasy art.

## 3. Recommended metadata schema for future cards

The current `style_word` field mixes aesthetic styles with visual attributes. For future iterations, keep the existing field for backward compatibility and consider gradually introducing clearer fields.

| Field | What it describes | Example values |
|---|---|---|
| `style_family` | Primary tattoo style | `minimalist`, `american_traditional`, `geometric`, `illustrative`, `blackwork`, `microrealism`, `japanese`, `neo_traditional`, `realism`, `ornamental`, `watercolor`, `surrealist` |
| `line_weight` | Stroke character | `fine`, `medium`, `bold`, `mixed` |
| `color_mode` | Ink/color treatment | `black_grey`, `color`, `limited_color`, `black_red` |
| `composition` | Arrangement and flow | `organic`, `symmetrical`, `flowing`, `radial`, `vertical`, `asymmetrical`, `framed`, `patchwork` |
| `detail_density` | Amount of visual information | `minimal`, `moderate`, `dense` |
| `motif_family` | Depicted subject | `botanical`, `animal`, `celestial`, `anatomical`, `mythological`, `portrait`, `lettering`, `geometric_symbol`, `religious_symbol`, `object` |
| `art_movement` | Historical/artistic influence | `art_nouveau`, `art_deco`, `renaissance`, `baroque`, `neoclassical`, `ukiyo_e`, `surrealism` |
| `symbolic_domain` | Cultural, religious, or mythological context when relevant | `christianity`, `hinduism`, `buddhism`, `jainism`, `islamic_art`, `celtic_history`, `greek_mythology`, `norse_mythology` |
| `placement` | Body area shown | `forearm`, `upper_arm`, `shoulder`, `chest`, `sternum`, `spine`, `leg`, `hand`, `multiple` |
| `image_status` | Asset production state | `planned`, `generated`, `reviewed`, `approved`, `rejected` |

### Data-model notes

- A card can have more than one motif, art influence, or symbolic domain. Consider arrays for fields that support multiple values.
- Keep `style_family` independent from `line_weight`, `color_mode`, and `composition`. A Japanese tattoo, for example, may also be bold, colorful, and flowing.
- The current field `color` is retained in the master table for compatibility. If migrating the code, map it to the new field `color_mode`.
- Avoid mapping every card labelled `bold` to one style family. Bold describes visual treatment; blackwork, Gothic lettering, and cybersigilism are distinct aesthetics.
- Cultural and religious references should be represented specifically and respectfully rather than grouped into one generic category.

## 4. Image-generation and asset consistency checklist

Use these guidelines when generating or sourcing the new card images so users compare the tattoo designs rather than incidental presentation differences.

- Use a consistent image aspect ratio and resolution across the deck.
- Keep image quality, lighting, background, and photography style consistent wherever possible.
- Make the tattoo the main visual subject; avoid prominent clothing, jewelry, props, or scenery competing for attention.
- Ensure the tattoo is visibly placed on skin or shown as a clear tattoo-flash design, depending on the card's intended presentation.
- Avoid illegible writing, malformed hands or anatomy, broken linework, and impossible placement on body contours.
- Make color, line weight, composition, and detail density visibly distinguishable where those attributes are intended to drive taste scoring.
- For religious, historical, or cultural motifs, verify the symbol's form and meaning before approving the image.
- Review images for skin realism, tattoo readability, cropping, visual similarity to other cards, and safe/appropriate presentation.
- Do not mark a generated image as approved until it has been visually reviewed.

### Suggested prompt template

> Professional editorial photograph of a finished **[tattoo concept]** tattoo placed on **[body location]**. **[Describe the defining tattoo style, line weight, color treatment, composition, shading, and relevant iconographic details.]** The tattoo is the clear focal point, follows the anatomy naturally, and uses clean, believable tattoo linework and skin texture. Consistent studio lighting, uncluttered background, high detail, realistic tattoo execution, no extra tattoos competing with the design, no watermark, no text overlays.

Adapt the prompt to the visual family. For a flash card, replace the body-placement language with a clean, isolated tattoo-flash presentation on a plain neutral background.

### Religious and spiritual imagery
- Christianity: crosses, sacred hearts, angels, doves, and other motifs selected by the user
- Hinduism: deity iconography and symbols, with accurate attributes and respectful presentation
- Buddhism: lotus, dharmachakra, and other context-appropriate motifs
- Jainism: the Jain cosmological emblem and related iconography
- Judaism: selected Jewish symbols where relevant to the user's preferences
- Sikhism: Khanda and other accurately represented symbols
- Islamic art: geometric and vegetal ornament; handle sacred text and religious imagery with care
- African traditional religions and cultural traditions: research specific communities and meanings rather than treating the continent's traditions as one category

Keep style, motif, art movement, and symbolic/cultural domain as separate classification dimensions. Religious or cultural cards should be optional in the taste discovery experience.

## 6. Taste-discovery experiment idea

Create small comparison sets with the **same subject rendered in different styles**. For example, show a serpent in illustrative, Japanese, neo-traditional, and realism styles. This helps distinguish a user's preference for the subject from their preference for the visual treatment.

Other useful comparison subjects include a flower, skull, portrait, moth, sacred symbol, or celestial motif. Keep the crop and presentation as consistent as possible across each set.
